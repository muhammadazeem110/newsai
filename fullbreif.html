Monitoring

Despite a ceasefire that has been in place since April 8, 2026, the situation on the ground remains highly unstable, with active military exchanges threatening to reignite full-scale war . In the most recent significant escalation, a U.S. F/A-18 Super Hornet disabled two Iranian-flagged oil tankers in the Gulf of Oman on May 8, 2026, as they attempted to breach an American blockade of Iranian ports . This U.S. action was a direct response to an earlier Iranian attack on three U.S. Navy destroyers in the strategic Strait of Hormuz, during which Iran launched missiles, drones, and fast-attack boats; the U.S. intercepted the assault and retaliated by striking Iranian military facilities in the area . While U.S. President Donald Trump has insisted the ceasefire is still "in effect," Iran has accused Washington of violating the truce and hampering diplomatic efforts, with Iranian forces launching retaliatory strikes before clashes temporarily ceased

Amid the ongoing hostilities, intensive backchannel diplomacy is underway, with Pakistan playing a key mediating role between Tehran and Washington . The United States has sent Iran a new proposal, delivered via Pakistani mediators, aimed at extending the truce and establishing a framework for negotiations to permanently end the war, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and address Iran's nuclear program . However, Iran has not yet reached a conclusion, stating it continues to review the proposal while dismissing American-imposed deadlines . The protracted conflict has already had severe global economic consequences, as Iran has largely blocked the Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments—since the war began, causing a dramatic spike in fuel prices and rattling world markets . The U.S. has imposed its own blockade of Iranian ports, and experts warn that if the strait remains closed, oil prices could surge beyond $167 per barrel, potentially triggering a global recession

The war has also widened beyond the direct U.S.-Iran confrontation, drawing in other fronts and regional actors. On the separate Lebanon front, Iran-backed Hezbollah has launched missiles and drones at Israeli military bases in retaliation for Israeli strikes on Beirut and southern Lebanon, where Lebanese authorities reported multiple civilian casualties . Additionally, the United Arab Emirates has confirmed intercepting missile and drone attacks believed to have originated from Iran, highlighting the growing danger to civilians across the Gulf region . Strategically, the U.S. now faces significant challenges as the war has not gone according to plan; intelligence reports indicate that Iran was not actively developing a nuclear weapon before the conflict began, and the U.S.-Israeli decapitation strategy failed to fracture the regime . Analysts suggest the likely endgame may involve an American retreat, leaving Iran with operational control over the Strait of Hormuz and a significantly enhanced deterrent posture, while the U.S. long-term military presence in the Gulf could be substantially reduced
